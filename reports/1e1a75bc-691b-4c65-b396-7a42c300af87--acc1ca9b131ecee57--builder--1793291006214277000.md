How it works:

- **The listener learns why it stopped.** `quiet()` in `signal_cc/entry/hooks.py` sends a different signal per reason:
  - SIGUSR1: a prompt typed at the Mac.
  - SIGUSR2: the session ended.
  - SIGTERM: anything else, such as a new turn or a prompt woken from Signal (same as before).
- **The listener turns that signal into an exit.** `adapters/processes.py` makes each signal a `Stopped(why)`, which still exits with status 0.
- **The notice goes out where the prompt is released.** `Conversation._ask` in `signal_cc/app/conversation.py` sends it through the Messenger port, just before it releases the prompt.
- **Only a message still waiting gets one.** The notice goes only if the message was sent and no other ending was recorded yet. An answered prompt, a timed-out one, or a listener replaced by a new turn sends nothing.
- **A failed notice is dropped.** The listener exits normally either way.
- **relay.log stays accurate.** It records `answered at the Mac` for a typed prompt and `session closed` when the session ends, instead of `quieted`. The other cases still log `quieted`.
- **Shared names.** `Stopped`, `AT_MAC` and `CLOSED` are new in `signal_cc/domain/model.py`, so both `adapters` and `app` can use them without breaking the layer rules. The notice wording is in `signal_cc/domain/texts.py` (`STOPPED`), and the README explains the notice and the new outcomes.

Decisions you might overturn:

- **Two outcomes, not one.** The item says relay.log records "answered at the Mac", but I log `session closed` when the session ends, because nobody answered at the Mac then and the outcome should stay truthful. Making both say `answered at the Mac` is a one-line change.
- **Listeners from the old version.** A listener started before this change has no handler for the new signals, so the new `quiet()` kills it outright. Its prompt then stays claimed until its timeout runs out. This only lasts until those old listeners are gone.
